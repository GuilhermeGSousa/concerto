# Who can go where

The manor splits the two players by a rule each of them can say in one
sentence, and the split moves through the night with the characters'
schedules. This is the answer to issue #758.

## The three kinds of room

| Zone | Rooms | The Guest | The Staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | Hall, drawing room, dining room, library, billiard room, conservatory, corridors, gallery, terrace, garden | Free | Free |
| Service | Kitchen, pantry, servants' hall, passage, back stairs, attic, cellar, lamp room, closet, yard, garage | Never | Free |
| Private | The bedrooms, dressing room, bathroom and study | Only while someone is in to answer the door | Only while nobody is in, or when the room has rung |

A room's zone is the zone of its doors in the level manifest; a case can change
a door's zone.

## The private rule

A private door is tried from outside by a hand on the handle:

- **The Guest** is let in while a character is in the room. An empty room that
  is not theirs stays shut: "Nobody answers."
- **The Staff** walks into an empty room to do it. With someone in, they wait
  to be rung for: "Someone is in."
- **A bell pulled in the room** raises its flag, and the Staff may enter while
  the flag is up. Reading the bell board drops the flag, so the Staff answers a
  bell before reading the board.
- **A player's own room**, the one they start the night in, is always theirs.
- **A key** to the door lets the Guest in whoever is or is not there.
- **Leaving** is never refused.
- A lock still holds: a locked private door needs its key or the key ring.

A character on their way between two places is in neither.

## What it does to the night

The same room belongs to each player at different hours. While its occupant is
in, the Guest can visit and talk and the Staff is kept out. Once the occupant
goes down to dinner, the Staff can search it and the Guest cannot.

So the players work for each other across the door:

| One player wants | The other does |
|---|---|
| The Staff in an occupied room | The Guest visits, and pulls the bell from inside |
| A room searched | The Guest keeps its occupant talking downstairs while the Staff goes in |
| Something out of a room the Guest cannot enter | The Staff takes it and sends it by the dumbwaiter, or carries it to a public room |
| Something out of the service wing | The Staff sends it up; the gallery stop is on the Guest's side |
| To hear a talk behind a closed private door | The Staff listens at it; the Guest in the room makes it change subject |

## Over the night

Nothing new is needed for the rule to move: schedules empty and fill the rooms.
A case adds the fixed changes it wants with what the schema already has: lock
windows on a door, a different starting state, a key placed as an item, and
which doors the key ring opens.

## How it reads

- The art kit papers the guest side and plasters the service side, and the
  manor's lamps are warm on the guest side and pale on the service side.
- A refusal says why in one line, in the player's half.
- The first-night card should carry the two sentences of the private rule once
  the manor has characters in it.

## Not built

- A door left standing open is walked through by anyone; the rule is asked
  only when a hand is on the handle.
- Knocking is not an action of its own, and a character does not react to
  being visited.
- The pantry's key cupboard is not a fixture; keys are items a case places.
